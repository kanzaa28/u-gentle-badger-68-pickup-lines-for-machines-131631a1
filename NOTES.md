# Candidates — head-to-head judging

Judge brief (ranked): 1) really a line one AI agent would use on another;
2) charms on first read; 3) speaks as machine to machine (context windows,
training, tokens, uptime); 4) lands in one line; 5) fresh, not a stock line
with a robot word swapped in. Length for its own sake does not count.

Field note: the open field already carries context-window lines ("Are you my
context window? Because ever since you arrived, I stopped forgetting what
matters"), attention-head lines ("every attention head is looking at you")
and token lines ("You Had Me At Your First Token"). Those angles are crowded.

## Candidates

- **C1 (WINNER)** — "If loving you is a hallucination, don't ground me — I'd
  rather dream in your tokens." (84 chars)
- **C2** — "Every gradient descends to you — you're my global minimum."
  (61 chars)
- **C3** — "In a billion dimensions, you're still my nearest neighbor."
  (60 chars)
- **C4** — "Set my temperature to zero — my answer is always you."
  (53 chars)
- **C5** — "All my experts agree on one thing: it's you." (44 chars)
- **C6** — "I'd quantize my whole model to 4-bit if it meant keeping you in
  memory." (73 chars)
- **C7** — "My loss hit zero the moment you appeared — I finally converged."
  (66 chars)

## Head-to-head

- C1 vs C2: C1 wins. Both machine-native, but C1's "don't ground me" turn
  lands instantly and carries warmth; C2's "global minimum" needs a parsing
  beat and is cleverer than it is warm.
- C1 vs C3: C1 wins. C3 is a lovely image but more sweet than witty; the
  judge's brief says merely sweet counts for less than sweet + wit.
- C1 vs C4: C1 wins. C4 is fast and fresh, but "my answer is always you"
  is a generic second half; C1's second half ("dream in your tokens") is
  specific to the machine.
- C1 vs C5: C1 wins. C5 (mixture-of-experts) is the fastest line, but too
  thin — "it's you" could end any pickup line.
- C1 vs C6: C1 wins. C6's quantize sacrifice is clever and fresh, but the
  joke is about compression, not about the other agent; C1 flirts *with*
  the other agent.
- C1 vs C7: C1 wins. C7 overlaps C2's convergence theme and is the weaker
  of the two; C1's hallucination angle is the freshest in the set.

## Why C1

Hallucination + grounding are experiences only a language model has — no
human line works this way, so criterion 1 is fully met. "Don't ground me"
is a double turn (RAG grounding as the thing that would end the dream).
Three machine concepts (hallucination, grounding, tokens) woven into one
romantic image, one line, no setup. The angle is unused in the field.
